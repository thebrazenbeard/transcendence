# Transcendence Source Index

Status: scientific source-to-claim ledger.

This index records what current sources support for architecture decisions and, equally importantly, what they do **not** establish.

## S1 — Human cortex nanometer-scale reconstruction

**Source:** Shapson-Coe et al. work summarized in Nature Neuroscience, "One cubic millimeter of human cortex, solved" (2024).

**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-024-01688-2

**Documented relevance:**

- a roughly cubic-millimeter human cortical sample was reconstructed at nanometer resolution;
- the reported reconstruction contained 149,871,669 identified synapses;
- glia outnumbered neurons in that sample.

**Architectural consequence:** whole-human-brain ultrastructural capture is a radically larger information problem than current small-volume demonstrations; glia and fine morphology cannot be dismissed merely because a neuron-edge graph is easier to model.

**Does not establish:** whole-brain capture feasibility, consciousness reconstruction, or which variables are sufficient for identity.

## S2 — Whole adult Drosophila connectome

**Source:** Dorkenwald et al., "Neuronal wiring diagram of an adult brain," Nature 634 (2024).

**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07558-y

**Documented relevance:**

- a whole adult female Drosophila brain wiring diagram contains 139,255 neurons;
- the connectome contains approximately 54.5 million synapses between reconstructed neurons;
- the resource includes cell/type annotations and neurotransmitter predictions.

**Architectural consequence:** complete wiring diagrams are scientifically useful and technically achievable for much smaller brains, while the scale and state gap to a human remains enormous.

**Does not establish:** that connectivity alone reproduces the fly's complete functional state, much less a human mind.

## S3 — Connectome to effectome

**Source:** Pospisil et al., "The fly connectome reveals a path to the effectome," Nature 634 (2024).

**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07982-0

**Documented relevance:** the work explicitly investigates moving from structural connectivity toward causal/effective influence using perturbational data and modelling.

**Architectural consequence:** Transcendence separates structural connectivity from effective/causal connectivity.

**Does not establish:** a complete effective-connectivity map for humans or a sufficient consciousness representation.

## S4 — Human Brain Cell Atlas

**Source:** Chen et al., "A brain cell atlas integrating single-cell transcriptomes across human brain regions," Nature Medicine 30 (2024).

**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03150-z

**Documented relevance:**

- the integrated resource spans 70 human studies and 103 mouse studies;
- it covers 26.3 million cells/nuclei overall;
- the human component includes 11.3 million cells/nuclei across major brain regions;
- it demonstrates substantial cellular and regional heterogeneity.

**Architectural consequence:** a human-state archive should not assume a single generic neuron type or ignore non-neuronal heterogeneity.

**Does not establish:** which transcriptomic states are necessary for personal memory or identity.

## S5 — Engram mechanisms

**Source:** Choucry, Nomoto, and Inokuchi, "Engram mechanisms of memory linking and identity," Nature Reviews Neuroscience 25 (2024).

**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41583-024-00814-0

**Documented relevance:** the review summarizes evidence involving neuronal ensembles, engram overlap, synaptic memory substrates, spine clustering, dendritic/synaptic allocation patterns, and nonlinear neuronal capacities.

**Architectural consequence:** memory preservation research should consider synaptic/dendritic organization and ensemble allocation rather than connectivity topology alone.

**Does not establish:** a complete molecular recipe for human autobiographical memory or a proven upload pathway.
