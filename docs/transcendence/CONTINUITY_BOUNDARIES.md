# Continuity Boundaries

Status: epistemic boundary specification.

## Why continuity must be decomposed

"Is it the same person?" bundles several different questions.

Transcendence separates them so evidence for one is not silently promoted into another.

## Behavioral continuity

Does the candidate behave similarly to the subject across controlled tests?

This can be measured, but may be achievable by imitation.

## Informational continuity

Does the candidate contain subject-specific memories, knowledge, values, skills, associations, and models with defensible provenance?

This is stronger than surface resemblance but still does not prove subjective persistence.

## Functional continuity

Does the candidate preserve/reconstruct the functional organization that generates subject-like cognition rather than merely replaying stored answers?

Evidence may include generalization, ablation, internal-state coherence, procedural performance, and causal tests.

## Causal-organization continuity

Does the reconstruction preserve the relevant causal relationships among state variables and processes?

This is a mechanistic claim requiring evidence beyond behavioral output.

## Temporal/process continuity

Was there an unbroken or deliberately overlapping causal process between biological and non-biological implementations—for example, a hypothetical gradual replacement process?

A process may have stronger temporal-continuity evidence than scan-and-reinstantiate, but this distinction does not by itself solve subjective identity.

## Subjective continuity

Did first-person experience persist as the same subject?

Current external science does not provide a generally accepted empirical test that can establish this across destructive substrate replacement.

Therefore:

`SUBJECTIVE_CONTINUITY = UNKNOWN`

unless future evidence supplies a defensible criterion.

## Copy problem

If one archive can instantiate two behaviorally and functionally equivalent candidates, both may inherit strong informational/functional links to the source.

That fact exposes why "has the same memories" cannot by itself settle numerical personal identity.

Transcendence must report the branching event rather than declaring one copy metaphysically privileged without evidence.

## Gradual replacement

Gradual replacement is a distinct research track because it may preserve ongoing causal/temporal organization differently from destructive scanning.

Potential evidence questions include:

- continuity of active state across replacement;
- overlap between biological and artificial causal participation;
- maintenance of memory access and self-model through transitions;
- absence of discontinuous reset;
- reversibility/rollback behavior.

Even a technically continuous process should be labeled `CONTINUITY_PRESERVATION_CANDIDATE` rather than subjective-continuity proof unless a future criterion justifies more.

## Operational rule

Every user-facing or scientific claim should identify which continuity dimension it means.

Avoid bare statements such as "the person survived" unless the project can specify and support the exact sense of survival.
