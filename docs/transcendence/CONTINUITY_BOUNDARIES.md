# Continuity Boundaries

Status: epistemic boundary specification.

## Why continuity must be decomposed

"Is it the same person?" bundles several different questions.

Transcendence separates them so evidence for one is not silently promoted into another.

## Operational events

The lineage model distinguishes what happened from what continuity claims are justified.

### BACKUP

Continuity-bearing state is preserved in an archive.

A backup is evidence preservation, not a running person and not continuity proof.

### RESTORE

Preserved state is instantiated into a functioning target.

A successful restore proves that an artifact could be instantiated under its declared criteria; it does not automatically prove identity with the biological source.

### SUCCESSOR

A resulting instance materially derives from a source person's archived state while stronger numerical/personal identity remains unestablished.

### FORK

Two or more descendants continue from a shared predecessor state.

They share lineage up to the branch point and accumulate distinct history afterward.

### MIGRATION

State/function moves across substrates through a controlled handoff.

### GRADUAL_TRANSFER

Biological and synthetic substrates overlap while causal function is progressively transferred, potentially through future bidirectional BCI.

These event types may coexist in one lineage graph.

## Biological continuity

Does the same living biological organism/process continue?

This is a distinct question from reconstruction on another substrate.

## Behavioral continuity

Does the candidate behave similarly to the subject across controlled tests?

This can be measured, but may be achievable by imitation.

## Informational / autobiographical continuity

Does the candidate contain subject-specific memories, knowledge, associations, skills, values, and life-history structure with defensible provenance?

This is stronger than surface resemblance but still does not prove subjective persistence.

## Psychological continuity

Are personality organization, preferences, values, relationships/person models, characteristic reasoning, emotional associations, goals, and self-model sufficiently preserved across the transition?

Psychological continuity can be investigated longitudinally and behaviorally but remains distinct from biological or phenomenal continuity.

## Functional continuity

Does the candidate preserve/reconstruct the functional organization that generates subject-like cognition rather than merely replaying stored answers?

Evidence may include generalization, ablation, internal-state coherence, procedural performance, and causal tests.

## Causal / neural continuity

Does the process preserve or defensibly reconstruct relevant causal organization, neural dynamics, effective connectivity, plastic state, or other mechanistic relationships at the resolution available?

This is a mechanistic claim requiring evidence beyond behavioral output.

A future BCI may contribute direct longitudinal evidence to this dimension, but neural measurement is still limited by its acquisition scope and interpretation.

## Temporal / process continuity

Was there an unbroken or deliberately overlapping causal process between biological and non-biological implementations—for example, a future gradual BCI-mediated replacement process?

A process may have stronger temporal-continuity evidence than scan-and-reinstantiate, but this distinction does not by itself solve subjective identity.

## Phenomenal / subjective continuity

Did first-person experience persist as the same subject?

Current external science does not provide a generally accepted empirical test that can establish this across destructive substrate replacement or non-destructive migration.

Therefore:

`PHENOMENAL_CONTINUITY = UNKNOWN`

unless future evidence supplies a defensible criterion.

## Copy and fork problem

If one archive can instantiate two behaviorally and functionally equivalent candidates, both may inherit strong informational/functional links to the source.

That fact exposes why "has the same memories" cannot by itself settle numerical personal identity.

Transcendence records the branch rather than declaring one descendant metaphysically privileged without evidence.

Lineage is therefore a graph, not necessarily one linear "real person" pointer.

## Gradual replacement / migration

Gradual replacement is a distinct research track because it may preserve ongoing causal/temporal organization differently from destructive scanning.

Potential evidence questions include:

- continuity of active state across replacement;
- overlap between biological and artificial causal participation;
- maintenance of memory access and self-model through transitions;
- BCI-measured continuity across handoff;
- whether replaced function remains causally integrated before and after substitution;
- absence or presence of discontinuous reset;
- reversibility/rollback behavior;
- fork points or periods of multiple independently continuing implementations.

Even a technically continuous process should be labeled a continuity-preservation candidate rather than subjective-continuity proof unless a future criterion justifies more.

## Claim matrix rule

Evidence must name the continuity dimension it supports.

Examples:

`BEHAVIORAL_CONTINUITY != PSYCHOLOGICAL_CONTINUITY_BY_DEFAULT`

`PSYCHOLOGICAL_CONTINUITY != BIOLOGICAL_CONTINUITY`

`FUNCTIONAL_CONTINUITY != PHENOMENAL_CONTINUITY_PROOF`

`CAUSAL_NEURAL_CONTINUITY != PHENOMENAL_CONTINUITY_PROOF`

`TEMPORAL_PROCESS_CONTINUITY != PHENOMENAL_CONTINUITY_PROOF`

## Operational rule

Every user-facing or scientific claim should identify which continuity dimension it means.

Avoid bare statements such as "the person survived" unless the project can specify and support the exact sense of survival.
